How to Make Your Money Last Until Payday

If the money in your account has to stretch for another week or two, it can feel stressful. The first step is to stop guessing. Work out what must be paid, what you need for everyday essentials and what is genuinely available until your next income arrives.

This is a short-term plan, not a reason to go without essentials. Keep food, necessary travel, medication, housing and essential bills in view. If there is not enough to cover them, seek help rather than trying to solve the gap with expensive borrowing.

1. Check the exact number of days until payday

Write down the date your next wages or benefits are due. Check whether it is definitely arriving on that date, and include any other income you are confident will reach your account. Do not count money that is only possible, such as unconfirmed overtime or a pending refund.

2. List everything that still needs to leave your account

Check your balance and pending transactions, then list direct debits, standing orders, rent or mortgage, Council Tax, travel and other essential payments due before payday. Remember card payments that have not yet cleared. This prevents you spending money that is already committed.

3. Work out your genuinely available amount

Use this simple calculation:

Current available balance
+ income arriving before payday
− bills and essential payments still due
− money reserved for essential travel, food and medication
= amount available for other spending

If the result is negative, or does not cover essentials, contact the relevant bill provider as soon as possible and ask what support or payment options are available. Free debt advisers can help you prioritise payments.

4. Divide food and flexible spending into weekly amounts

Set aside what your household needs for groceries, toiletries and cleaning products until payday. Plan simple meals around food you already have, check cupboards and freezer before shopping, and make a list before going to the supermarket.

Divide any remaining flexible amount by the number of days or weeks left. A daily or weekly limit is easier to follow than one large balance. Keep a little aside for an unavoidable cost if you can.

5. Pause spending that can safely wait

  • Pause non-essential online shopping and subscriptions you do not need right now.
  • Use food already in the freezer or cupboards before buying more.
  • Choose free or low-cost family activities for the short period.
  • Check whether planned purchases can wait until after payday.
  • Do not cancel essential insurance or services without understanding the consequences.

6. Avoid quick fixes that make next month harder

Payday loans, high-cost credit, cash advances and repeated use of an overdraft can make the next payday even tighter. Before borrowing, speak to a free, independent debt-advice service and ask providers about hardship support or affordable arrangements.

7. Make next month easier with a payday routine

When your next income arrives, cover priority bills first, set aside money for weekly food and transport, and put a small amount towards annual costs if affordable. Keep irregular expenses in a separate pot so that Christmas, school costs or car repairs do not all land on one payday.

It can also help to ask bill providers to move payment dates to just after payday, where they allow it. Keep a simple list of payment dates so you know what your balance needs to cover.

Where to get free help in the UK

If you cannot afford essentials or are falling behind on bills, you are not expected to work it out alone. Contact Citizens Advice, StepChange or National Debtline for free, independent guidance. MoneyHelper also provides information on managing money and debt. If your income or family circumstances have changed, check whether you may be entitled to support using GOV.UK's benefits calculators.

Frequently asked questions

What should I pay first before payday?

Protect essentials and understand which bills are priority commitments. If you cannot pay everything, get free debt advice promptly and contact providers rather than ignoring the problem.

How do I budget when I have only a small amount left?

Calculate what is left after pending bills and essential needs, then divide the genuinely available amount across the days until payday. Be realistic about food and necessary travel.

Should I use a credit card to get to payday?

Borrowing can make the next month harder, especially if you cannot repay it in full. Consider speaking to a free debt adviser before taking on more credit.

How can I stop running short every month?

Track actual spending for a month, include annual and irregular costs, and set aside weekly food and spending amounts on payday. If essential costs are higher than income, check benefit entitlement and seek free advice.
