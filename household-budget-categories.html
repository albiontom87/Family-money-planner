Household budget checklist

Household Budget Categories: What to Include in a UK Family Budget

A household budget is only useful if it includes the costs you actually pay. It is easy to remember rent, energy and food, but forget school trips, car repairs, birthdays or annual renewals until the money is needed.

Use this checklist to build a complete picture of your family spending. You do not need every category: keep the ones that apply to your household and add any costs unique to you.

Quick tip: Look through two or three months of bank statements and a full year of annual bills. Your transactions are often a better checklist than trying to remember everything.

1. Home and household bills

  • Rent or mortgage payments
  • Council Tax
  • Gas, electricity and heating
  • Water and sewerage
  • Broadband, landline and mobile phones
  • TV Licence and streaming subscriptions
  • Buildings and contents insurance
  • Essential repairs, maintenance and household supplies

2. Food and everyday essentials

  • Supermarket groceries and top-up shops
  • Packed lunches and school snacks
  • Toiletries, nappies and personal care
  • Cleaning products and laundry supplies
  • Pet food and routine pet costs
  • Meals out, takeaway and coffees

Decide whether toiletries and cleaning products come from the food budget or a separate household category. Either approach is fine; the important thing is not to count them twice or leave them out.

3. Transport and getting to work

  • Petrol, diesel or electric vehicle charging
  • Car finance, lease or loan payments
  • Car insurance, road tax and breakdown cover
  • MOT, servicing, tyres, repairs and parking
  • Bus, train and other public transport fares
  • Work travel that is not reimbursed

4. Children, school and family life

  • Childcare, nursery and after-school clubs
  • School meals, uniforms, shoes and supplies
  • School trips, clubs, sports and hobbies
  • Children's haircuts and seasonal clothing
  • Birthday parties, presents and family days out
  • Child maintenance or other family commitments

5. Health, personal and insurance costs

  • Prescriptions, dental and optical costs
  • Health, life, pet or other insurance premiums
  • Haircuts and personal care
  • Clothing and footwear for adults
  • Gym, sports and wellbeing costs

6. Debt and financial commitments

  • Credit card and store card minimum payments
  • Personal loans and car finance
  • Buy now, pay later instalments
  • Overdraft charges and agreed repayments
  • Any arrears or repayment arrangements

Make sure you know which payments are priority commitments and get free advice if you are struggling. Do not ignore missed-payment notices or borrow more simply to make a budget appear balanced.

7. Annual and irregular expenses

These costs are often the missing piece in a family budget. Write down the expected yearly total, divide it by 12 and set aside a monthly amount where possible.

  • Christmas, birthdays and other celebrations
  • Holiday travel and spending money
  • Car insurance, MOT and annual memberships
  • School uniform replacement and term-start costs
  • Home repairs, appliances and furniture
  • Seasonal clothing, shoes and equipment
  • Professional fees, licences or renewals

8. Savings and future plans

  • Emergency savings
  • Holiday and days-out funds
  • Car replacement or home improvement savings
  • Children's savings or future costs
  • Extra debt repayments, if affordable and appropriate

How to turn the checklist into a working budget

  1. Start with take-home income. Use the amount paid into your account, not gross salary.
  2. Add fixed bills. Record amounts and payment dates.
  3. Estimate variable costs. Use recent spending rather than an unrealistic ideal.
  4. Convert annual costs into monthly pots. Divide yearly totals by 12.
  5. Check what remains. Make sure there is a realistic amount for flexible spending and a small buffer if possible.
  6. Review monthly. Update prices and remove costs that no longer apply.

Common budget categories people forget

School photos, gifts, parking, prescription charges, takeaway coffees, app subscriptions, pet treatment, replacement phone chargers and small home repairs can all add up. Check bank statements for repeat payments and keep a short “miscellaneous” category for genuinely unpredictable small costs.
