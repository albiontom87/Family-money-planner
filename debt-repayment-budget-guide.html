How to Budget to Pay Off Debt in the UK

Debt can make a household budget feel impossible, especially when payments fall on different dates. A clear list of balances, interest rates and minimum payments is a better starting point than trying to tackle everything at once.

Make the plan fit your household. Costs, income and priorities differ between families. Use the ideas that apply to you and adjust the numbers to your own circumstances.

1. Protect essential bills and priority commitments

Make sure you understand rent or mortgage arrears, Council Tax, energy, court fines and other priority debts. The consequences of missing payments differ, so do not treat every debt as interchangeable. If you are at risk of losing your home or essential services, get advice urgently.

2. Make a complete list of debts

Record each creditor, balance, interest rate, minimum payment, due date and whether the account is up to date. Include credit cards, overdrafts, personal loans, store cards and buy-now-pay-later agreements. Check statements so the figures are accurate.

3. Work out what you can realistically afford

Build a household budget using take-home income and essential costs first. Include food, travel, childcare and irregular expenses. The amount left after essentials is not automatically all available for debt repayment; leave a realistic allowance for normal life and a small buffer if possible.

4. Understand common repayment methods

The avalanche method puts extra payments towards the debt with the highest interest rate while maintaining required payments on others. The snowball method targets the smallest balance first for a visible early win. Both need to fit your circumstances and should not come before priority obligations.

5. Contact creditors if you cannot keep up

If you cannot afford contractual payments, contact creditors and explain your situation. Do not promise an amount your budget cannot sustain. A free debt adviser can help you understand options and communicate with creditors.

6. Be cautious about debt solutions and fees

Debt management plans, individual voluntary arrangements, debt relief orders and bankruptcy have different eligibility rules and long-term consequences. Do not pay a company before understanding free alternatives and getting independent advice.

7. Review progress monthly

Update balances, check interest and charges, and adjust your plan if income or essential costs change. Celebrate progress, but avoid taking on new borrowing to make the repayment plan look better.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay the smallest debt first?

The snowball method can build motivation, while the avalanche method may reduce interest costs. Priority debts and affordability come first; seek advice if unsure.

Can I still save while paying off debt?

A small buffer may help avoid new borrowing, but the right balance depends on interest, arrears and essential costs. Free debt advice can help you decide.

Where can I get free debt advice in the UK?

You can contact StepChange, National Debtline or Citizens Advice for free, independent guidance.
